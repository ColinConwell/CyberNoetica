# Design Principles

## Codebase Organization

- Intuitiveness: The codebase should be human-readable and human-sensible, organized in a way that is easy to navigate, easy to understand, and easy to modify. Components should be written with an internal logic that maps meaningfully to the data structures they represent or interact with. Especially key components should be implemented to provide comprehensive, well-designed, and informative representations of their internal states, behaviors, interactions, and affordances (i.e. what they can be used to do). Redundancy (e.g. a convenience function that recapitulates a static class function) is not only acceptable, but encouraged when it serves the purpose of enhancing intuition by way of heuristic, pedagogical, or otherwise practical understanding of the codebase and the system it instantiates.
- Modularity: The codebase should be designed and organized to allow for fluid manipulation, reuse, or replacement of components and operations wherever they are used (upstream or downstream).
- Hierarchy: Components and operations should be implemented in a nested, hierarchical fashion, increasing in complexity and detail as they are nested deeper within the codebase, but linked meaningfully to the base components they are derived from, and / or the components they compose with. Another sense of hierarchy should be in the form of "progressive complexity" and "curricular exposure", wherein (for example) both the UI / UX flows and codebase allow users and developers alike to interact with and update the system with more or less advanced controls or development tools based on their familiarity and expertise.
- Extensibility: The codebase should be designed to allow for easy extension and modification of components and operations, providing clear hooks, plugin points, bridges, abstraction layers, and other mechanisms to facilitate the integration of new (internal or external) components and operations.
- Verifiability: Extensive programmatic and manual testing (e.g. with playwright) should be integrated and deployed whenever key components are implemented or modified. Validation and verification targets should be both low-level (e.g. unit tests) and high-level (e.g. end-to-end tests) to ensure the system is both correct and complete. Simulations of key data or interactions should be devised regularly to test the system's behavior and competence in both standard cases and edge scenarios.
- Internal, iterative self-improvement: As the intelligence inherent to our system improves over time both from development and the learning of new abstractions / skills, we should allow for a form of introspection / reflection / auto-reflexivity, wherein the system can self-clone, self-inspect, and propose modifications (in evolutionary terms, mutations) to its own codebase, running tests and validation protocols to continually refine its own implementation.
- Parsimony (An Aesthetic of Elegance + Form following Function): Perhaps the most important of the above principles, the foundational intent of our design philosophy and developmental protocols should be to achieve maximal performance with minimal complexity. We believe that parsimony (the optimal compression ratio that balances adapative performance with resource efficiency) is the key to general intelligence (defined as adaptation to new environments through rapid abstraction and skill acquisition) in biological systems. The artifical intelligence we will build in this project should reflect this same principle wherever and whenever it is possible to do so.
